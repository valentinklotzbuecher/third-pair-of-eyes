# Independent literature

How the review checks a planning value, a target effect or an external claim against
the literature beyond the source the product cites. Adapted from the upstream
protocol. The depth is proportional: a planning value that barely moves N needs its
cited source checked; a value N is sensitive to, or the target effect, needs the
literature around it.

## Define the propositions first

Translate what the product relies on into propositions a source can test:

- the control-group rate, mean, SD or event rate in a comparable population, setting,
  endpoint definition and time frame;
- the size of the treatment effect from comparable interventions, and whether
  published estimates are selected (small early trials overstate effects);
- the minimal clinically important difference for the endpoint;
- correlations, ICCs, dropout rates and recruitment rates in comparable studies;
- a regularity the product treats as established.

Do not begin with a list of well-known works and fit them to the proposition.

## Where to look

For each proposition, as far as its weight warrants:

1. systematic reviews and meta-analyses of the parameter (Cochrane, other reviews),
   and their selection-corrected estimates where reported;
2. previous trials of the same or similar interventions, including registered trials
   that never reported (ClinicalTrials.gov, the EU Clinical Trials Register, the WHO
   ICTRP, the Swiss SNCTP portal);
3. cohort and registry data for control rates in the population;
4. methodological sources for the endpoint (validation studies, MCID estimates);
5. pilot data the product cites, with their size.

## Vet each work

Record: identity from the document itself; work type and design; population, setting
and endpoint definition; the pages read; whether it supports, contradicts or qualifies
the proposition; its independence from the product's cited source; and whether it has
been corrected, retracted or contradicted by later work.

## Independence

- **Independent**: different data and design.
- **Partly independent**: shares some data or sources but brings substantial evidence
  of its own.
- **Dependent**: the same trial, cohort or data source (a secondary analysis of the
  trial the product already cites; a meta-analysis dominated by it).
- **Unknown**: not yet established.

Only the first two can corroborate. Several papers from one trial are one piece of
evidence.

## Acquisition block

When a required work cannot be obtained, log the citation, the proposition it must
test, the routes tried, any substitute and why it falls short, and what the operator
must supply. A statement resting on it stays `INTERIM` until the operator answers, then
`unverified-here`.
